How to use this

You do not need to know music theory. Press Play and listen. The page keeps a slow loop going. If Autopilot is on, it will gently change the loop while you leave it alone.

First buttons

Play starts the sound. Press it again to stop. The space bar does the same thing, unless you are typing in a box.

Record saves what you hear as a sound file, and a video of the moving background. It stops by itself after 30 minutes.

Plate only hides every control so you only see the moving picture. Move the mouse to the top of the screen, or tap the top on a phone, and the bar comes back. Show player, next to Help, brings the controls back.

Color changes the look of the whole page. The four squares are shortcuts: blue, green, amber, and grey. Grey stays grey. It does not turn blue or green.

Speed and groove

BPM is how fast the loop is, in beats per minute. It starts at 122 and can go from 112 to 132. Lower is slower.

Swing only nudges the hi-hats so they sit a little late. 0 is straight. Higher feels looser. It does not move the kick.

The big grid

Each row is one sound: kick, hi-hat, open hat, snare, rim, sub, chord, and plate. Each square is one moment in the loop. There are 16 moments, and then it repeats.

A bright outline shows the moment you are hearing right now. It walks from left to right.

Click a square to turn that hit on or off. Drag a square up to make it more likely when a new pattern is made, or down to make it less likely. The fill shows that amount, from 0 to 100. Zero means "never pick this square."

New pattern builds a fresh loop. Each square is on with the chance shown by its weight. The page starts with weight only on the notes already in the loop, at 92. Every other square starts at 0, so a new pattern stays close to that loop. The same roll happens when Autopilot writes a new pattern at the end of the Bars length. That length starts at 16. The chord stab's reverb is rendered four bars before the change. The Pattern weight is the chance that rewrite happens. Clear empties every square and sets every amount to zero.

Volume and mute

Vol is how loud that sound is, from 0 to 100. Len is how long the note lasts, in milliseconds. A millisecond is a thousandth of a second, so 420 is a short thump and 1800 is a long ring.

Mute switches that sound off without moving the volume slider. Turn Mute off and the sound comes back at the same loudness. The plate starts muted in volume, at 0, so the picture moves but you do not hear the plate until you turn it up.

Chords, in plain words

Root is the home note. It starts on D. The low sub and the plate stay on that home note.

The Chord menu moves the chord stab up or down to a nearby note that still fits the key. Harmonise should stay on for that. If you turn Harmonise off, New chord can jump to a note that does not fit, which sounds stranger. Minor 9 and sus on the home note do not move the stab. They are the same place as the home note.

Under Chord shapes every chord type has a row: Open 5, Minor 7, High 5, Minor, Sus, Minor 9, and Major. The outlined row is the one playing now. Click a square to choose the next chord. Click it again to clear that row. Drag a square to share the 100 with the square beside it. It starts as Open 5, then Minor 7, then High 5, then back. The other rows start empty, so they stay out of the loop until you click one. An empty row is skipped.

Echo, filter, and space

Delay time picks how long the echo waits, in musical lengths such as a dotted eighth. Feedback is how much of the echo is sent back in, from 20 to 88. Higher is wetter. It will not be pushed past 75 by Autopilot, so it does not turn into noise.

Cutoff is the filter, in hertz. Lower sounds darker, higher sounds brighter. It starts at 680. Its weight starts at 20, about 20 hertz of drift. Turn that weight up and the filter moves farther and faster. Interpolate makes the filter glide. Turn it off and the filter jumps.

Most other sliders are simply 0 to 100: how much echo is sent, how much reverb, how much warmth, how wide the stereo image is. You can turn them until it sounds right. The number is a position on that slider, not a special unit.

Autopilot

Leave this on if you want the page to keep the jam moving. A ticked box is allowed to wander. The small slider under it is how far it may wander, from 0 to 100. Pattern, chord, and muteouts wait until 16 bars have finished. The filter and the effect knobs move on every step.

It does not touch your volumes, note lengths, home note, speed, delay time, or drive. Those stay where you put them.

Saving

Sign up with email and a password. After you are signed in, Save stores this loop, and Share copies a link you can send. Sign out is there too. The email boxes hide once you are in.
